Buyer beware at DCH Toyota Wappingers

Hidden optional items, omitted cheaper warranty options, improper car inspections, and repairs done by sales staff instead of technicians.

In May 2026 I bought a Toyota Gold Certified 2022 Toyota Tundra from DCH Toyota in Wappingers Falls. It has been my worst car buying experience ever. I have spent dozens of hours and countless awkward conversations trying to get what I paid for.

The sales manager, Daryl Callahan, has been particularly nasty. He has been dishonest and condescending at every step. But the issues run deeper than one person. There is a built in culture of taking advantage of the customer. Hundreds of dollars of useless "optional" items that are not identified until signing, a warranty switched from what I asked for, and a disregard for the procedures Toyota lays out, like the Gold Certified inspection.

It turns out the truck I bought was actually Daryl's own trade in. He knew about many of the issues with it, and it still made it past the 160 point Gold Certified inspection.

I reached out to Toyota corporate. They told me that all decisions are up to the dealership, and if I do not agree with the decision I can look for solutions outside the company. Cool. So here we are.

I am not sure why this dealership did not do the right thing. If they had fixed these issues without hesitation I would have been a customer for life, because I would know I would be taken care of. Instead I am spending my time building this site. The cost to fix my issues was so small compared to the cost of just one or two people seeing this site.

ISSUE 01

Make sure you ask for the third-party warranty options. They are longer, cheaper, and exactly the same level of care. I saved $500 and got 24 more months.

They will not tell you about the cheaper one unless you ask.

I have gotten a 10 year, 100,000 mile warranty on my last six cars, and I expected the same here. When I paid the deposit, I wrote the 10 year warranty on the sheet and circled it. Unfortunately I did not receive a copy. That was before I realized I had to keep every little thing from them to use as evidence.

When I showed up at signing, there was a 7 year warranty on the list. I would never have agreed to that. I was about to walk out on the deal, because it is simply not what I agreed to. Only then did they reveal that there is a 9 year warranty that is exactly the same, and it is $500 cheaper. It is just from a third party. I saved $500 and got 24 more months, and I only got it because I pushed back.

Later they tried to convince me that the circled 10 year item on the deposit sheet was my offer, which they had countered. That is simply not true. At first I gave them the benefit of the doubt that the whole thing was a misunderstanding. After everything else I have been through with them, I do not think it was.

ISSUE 02

Watch out for last-minute "optional" charges folded into signing. Cost me $800 more.

Ask if the sticker price includes any optional items.

When I look back at the documents I was encouraged to quickly sign at closing, I realized I was charged $798 plus tax, about $863, for two separate items, PULSE and NITRO. The total price of the truck was the same as the sticker, but these items were left off the sticker. Essentially the sticker is inflated so they can tack these on at the end. You are allowed to refuse them. I wish I had caught it at the time. In my case they added the charge when the item was not even installed. Their own WE OWE slip, written the same day, says so.

The two "optional" items on my bill were PULSE and NITRO.

  • PULSE, $399. A small module that makes the third brake light flash when you brake. Unnecessary: no law requires it. Markup: the same modules sell for $6 to $25 online. Legal to refuse: yes, the FTC says legal to refuse.
  • NITRO, $399. Nitrogen in the tires instead of air. Unnecessary: air is already 78 percent nitrogen, and Consumer Reports says do not pay for it. Markup: tire shops charge about $5 a tire. Legal to refuse: yes, the FTC says legal to refuse.

ISSUE 03

Toyota's 160-point inspection not done by a technician, many issues missed

Summary

Part of the Gold Certified process is a detailed inspection of every car, signed off by several people, to make sure it is up to Toyota's standards. Toyota's official 160-Point Quality Assurance Inspection Check Sheet requires three signatures: a "Required Technician Signature" on a line labeled "Toyota Trained Technician," a "Required Manager Signature," and a Used Car Manager. The dealership's own website says "A rigorous 160-point inspection is completed on every gold certified Toyota" (their program page).

The checklist I was provided shows all three lines signed by the same person. The dealership's own staff page lists that person as a Sales Manager, not a technician. Even though the form requires the signee to confirm they are a technician.

According to this document, a technician never looked at my truck before it was Gold Certified. This is particularly troubling considering every box was marked as passing, even though I had many issues with the vehicle, including one that was unsafe. I have seen this pattern of neglecting advertised protocols when they are inconvenient throughout my dealings with this dealership.

ISSUE 05

The hands-free tailgate drop does not work from the key fob or the side button. Manager misleading about function.

Summary

I told Daryl Callahan, the General Sales Manager at DCH Wappingers Falls Toyota, that the rear tailgate does not drop with the Toyota brand tonneau cover installed. Rather than investigate, he told me the button only unlocks the latch, so it is working correctly.

This is incorrect. The fob button itself shows the tailgate down, and Toyota's Owner's Manual says: "Press and hold the switch. Tailgate opens by gravity."

I told him that if he could show me documentation that the Toyota cover is supposed to stop the gate from dropping, I would drop the issue. He could not point me to anything, so I did the research myself. Toyota's build sheet shows this cover is a genuine Toyota part, sold new on this truck by DCH Wappingers Falls Toyota itself. Toyota's own installation instructions for it end with a check the installer must complete: "Tailgate should open and close without interference." Mine does not. The dealership still will not acknowledge this as an issue and maintains it works as intended.

This is one example. I have been intentionally misled many times, to the point where I no longer feel comfortable working with this man.
